# Visible usefulness and original evidence

Source reviewed 2026-10-13: full English auto-generated transcript of Edward Sturm, “Google’s Secret ‘Content Effort’ Score: Here’s What It Looks For” (2026-10-12), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T2ZMC4utIBQ . This is editorial guidance, not proof of a ranking formula. Cross-check against Google’s people-first content guidance: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content .

## Apply when planning, writing or reviewing content

Judge effort by useful evidence and functionality visible on the finished page. Do not substitute word count, production time, decorative polish or human-versus-AI labels for usefulness. Templates and automation can serve users when grounded in distinct, verified inputs and thoughtful review.

Give the primary answer, verdict or action near the beginning; place supporting detail where readers can find it. Remove background that delays the answer. Do not lengthen pages just to hit a target.

Identify the page’s original contribution: firsthand observations, owned data, experiments, explicit comparisons, carefully supported analysis, practical edge cases, or a useful tool. Citation and superficial rewriting alone do not create additional value. For programmatic/location pages, check that each page has substantive local evidence and a distinct purpose; changing place names is insufficient.

Use authentic screenshots, photographs, demonstrations and documented methods where relevant. Custom illustrations can explain concepts, but generated images, altered stock images and operator logos are not evidence of firsthand product testing. Never invent experience, results, testimonials or credentials to meet this standard. Distinguish researched information from tested observations.

For reviews, explain what was evaluated, how, when, what happened, and limitations. For SweepsDuke specifically: an account signup, demo session, real-money purchase and prize redemption are different activities. Do not imply one proves another. Disclose the basis of scores and cite dated operator terms for reward, age and location claims. State pages need state-specific sources; news needs verification and useful context beyond paraphrasing a feed.

Where discussion helps, favor detailed, attributable perspectives with evidence and counterpoints. Do not require comments or discussion for every page.

## Short editorial check

1. Can the intended reader immediately find the answer or next step?
2. What distinct useful contribution does this page add?
3. What authentic evidence supports its observations or testing claims?
4. What decision-relevant details go beyond a generic summary?
5. Where appropriate, are conclusions supported by meaningful discussion and limitations?

Treat missing evidence as a concrete improvement opportunity, not a reason to fabricate it. Apply only relevant questions; a contact page or narrow factual lookup does not need a research report.

## Claims and measurement limits

The video discusses leaked fields named contentEffort and ugcDiscussionEffortScore, and explicitly says their live ranking use is unknown. Do not present a measurable Google “effort score,” a weighting, ranking guarantee, or a way to access such a score. Rater assessments inform evaluation of search systems; they do not directly set page rankings.

The quoted 2016 Google presentation is historical evidence, not a complete explanation of current search. Do not assert a deterministic bounce-rate penalty, that GA4 engagement metrics feed rankings, or that Chrome records every interaction for this purpose. Use Search Console and site analytics to diagnose user needs and measure improvements, not to manufacture engagement signals.

This guidance complements technical SEO, schema, accessibility and lawful sourcing. For technical-only tasks, load it only when content usefulness affects the task; do not turn an API lookup or sitemap fix into an unsolicited content audit.
